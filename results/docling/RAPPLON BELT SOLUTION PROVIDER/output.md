## KEY POINTS: TENSIONING

## Tensioning of elastic belts

Elastic belts show a wide range of recommended working tension. The optimal tension depend on:

- -Belt dimension
- -Machine design
- -Goods to be transported

The various possibilities regarding applications and belt dimensions cannot be covered by specific rules. Nevertheless, for the combination of belt width and working tension the chart below can be used as a guideline based on experiences.

| Belt width    | Recommended elongation   | Maximum elongation   |
|---------------|--------------------------|----------------------|
| < 50 mm       | 3%                       | 6%                   |
| 50 - 100 mm   | 2.5 %                    | 5%                   |
| 100 - 200 mm  | 2%                       | 4%                   |
| 200 - 400 mm  | 1.5 %                    | 2%                   |
| 400 - 600 mm  | 1%                       | 1.5%                 |
| 600 - 1200 mm | 0.5 %                    | 1%                   |

It is easy to define the correct RAPPLON ®  belt in the Boxfolding Industry by asking 3 questions:

1. Food Grade or Non Food Grade?
2. QuickSplice or Classic?

## KEY POINTS: BELT WIDTH

Bobst used flanged pulleys in the past to hold the belts in place.

In order to reduce costs, the wavy shaped guide rails became standard on most machines - very challenging for the belt edges.

The original belt width defined by Bobst is equal to the free space between the guide rails.

This does not leave any room for tracking or fabrication tolerances.

We recommend to fabricate belts by 1-2 mm narrower than specified -especially the lower transport belts.

- Air is transported in the grooves around the pulley
- No lateral escape air under pressure = no noise
- No reduction of belt pressure
